- designs M3's systems (the combat phase, attack and block declarations, combat damage, extra
  turns, phases and steps) so that every combat rule and keyword in the rules coverage map fits
  later (section 4.1);
- shows how the design handles the torture tests assigned to M3 and those of later milestones

  sickness and `can_tap_for_cost` versus convoke and crew (302.6; docs/decisions.md, 2026-10-21,

and, before it was saved, updated for M2.11 (the caster fuzz seat, the test that every gap has an

## 2. Architecture review

What combat needs from the architecture, and whether it fits. Each item is written up in
design.md (§17, provisional) in M3.0.

1. **Combat state.** A combat phase's facts (who attacks what, who blocks what, blocked or
   unblocked, the first-strike step) are pure data in `TurnState.combat`, created as a combat
   phase begins and cleared as it ends (511.3). Fits.
2. **Declarations are constraint problems.** 508.1c-d and 509.1b-c ask for the declaration that
   obeys the most requirements without breaking a restriction. Design 3.2 compiles the state
   into a pure model carried by the decision's space, so validation, the UI and agents all see
   the same rules (question Q2). Fits, with a search whose worst case is exponential (risk 3.2).
3. **Phases need an identity** (500.8-500.10, 506.8d, 724.2, "that combat"). `TurnState.segments`
   is a list of `(phase, step)` tuples, which can't tell two consecutive combat phases apart.
   Design 3.10 makes a segment a small value with a phase number (question Q1). This changes a
   built design (design.md §4), so it needs sign-off.
4. **Two damage steps** (510.4) are a step inserted into the current phase, in
   `TurnState.segments` like any extra step (500.9). Fits.
5. **Damage results.** Lifelink and deathtouch plug into M2's 120.3 table in `DamageDealt.apply`
   (design.md §5, "Resolution and where a spell goes"). Lifelink's life gain stays a result recorded inside the damage event until
   M6, when replacement effects that modify results (120.4c) need them as events of their own
   (question Q8). Fits.
6. **Look-back.** Relentless Assault reads "creatures that attacked this turn" (508.6). M3 builds
   it as the first query of M4's history derived from the event log (design.md §17.9;
   question Q6). Fits.
7. **Later combat mechanics** (section 4.1): triggers on attacking, blocking and combat damage
   (M4) read M3's events, which carry everything 508.3, 509.3 and 510.3a need; planeswalkers
   and battles (M4) are attack targets the target type already allows; control changes,
   phasing and regeneration (M5, M6, M9) remove creatures from combat through the same
   function as leaving the battlefield; banding (M9) plugs into `combat_damage_assigner` and a
   band field. Nothing found that can't fit.

## 3. Design

**(design.md)** marks what M3.0 writes up, provisionally, in design.md §17.

### 3.1 Combat state **(design.md)**

`TurnState.combat: CombatState | None`, a mutable `slots` dataclass, pure data:

- `phase`: the number of the combat phase it belongs to (3.10);
- `defending_players`: fixed as the combat phase begins (506.2; 802.2: every opponent in a
  multiplayer game, a deliberate non-parameter, docs/decisions.md 2026-10-18);
- `attackers: dict[ObjectId, AttackRecord]`, in declaration order:
  - `target: AttackTarget | None`: `AttackPlayer(player)` or `AttackPermanent(obj)` (a
    planeswalker or battle, M4); `None` once that permanent leaves combat (506.4c);
  - `declared`: declared as an attacker (508.1) or put onto the battlefield attacking (508.4:
    never "attacked");
  - `blocked: bool | None`: unknown until blockers are declared, then fixed (509.1h);
  - room: `chosen_target` for reselection (508.7a), `band` for banding (702.22, M9);
- `blockers: dict[ObjectId, BlockRecord]`: the attackers each blocks, in order, and `declared`;
- `attacks_happened`: some creature was declared or put onto the battlefield attacking this
  combat (508.8 reads this, not the current attackers);
- `first_strike: FirstStrikeRecord | None`: the attackers and blockers that had first strike or
  double strike as the first combat damage step began (510.4, 702.4b, 702.7b).

Invariants (`resolve.state.invariants`): combat only during a combat phase; attackers are
creatures on the battlefield controlled by the active player; blockers are creatures on the
battlefield controlled by defending players; a blocker blocks only attackers in combat.
`PlayerView.combat` shows it all (public). The scenario builder sets it
(`Scenario.attacking(...)`, `Scenario.blocking(...)`).

### 3.2 Declarations as constraint models: `ChooseAssignment` **(design.md §17.8)**

A declaration is compiled from the state into a pure model, which the decision's space carries:

```
ChooseAssignment(
    items,         # the creatures that may attack (or block), as ObjectOptions
    choices,       # per item, what it may pick: attack targets, or attackers to block
    maxima,        # per item, how many picks: 1 to attack; blocks_allowed to block (5)
    constraints,   # declaration-wide restrictions, a closed vocabulary
    requirements,  # each: an item and the picks that obey it
    costly,        # (item, choice) pairs that need a cost paid (508.1h, 509.1d)
)
answer: Assignment(picks)   # per item, strictly increasing choice indices (canonical)
```

- **Compiling** (`resolve.rules.combat`): an item and a choice are present only if
  `legality.allowed(AttackAction(creature, target))` or `allowed(BlockAction(blocker,
  attacker))` says so (3.4). Every restriction that concerns one creature, or one creature and
  one target or attacker, is a prohibition there: tapped, summoning sick, defender, "can't
  attack or block unless", evasion, protection. What concerns the whole declaration becomes a
  constraint: `NoneOrAtLeast(choice, n)` (menace: 0 or at least 2 blockers), `NotAlone(item)`
  (Mogg Flunkies), `AtMost(n)` (Silent Arbiter). A restriction the vocabulary can't express
  raises `NotImplementedRule` when compiled.
- **Requirements** (`Requirement(item, picks)`): "attacks each combat if able" obeyed by any
  target; "blocks [this creature] if able" (Prized Unicorn, Lure) obeyed by blocking it. Each
  instance counts once (508.1d, 509.1c).
- **Validation is the space's own** (`ChooseAssignment.validate`, no state needed): picks are
  in range and canonical; every constraint holds; and the number of requirements obeyed is at
  least the most any declaration *without costly picks* obeys while satisfying the constraints
  (508.1d: a player isn't required to pay a cost to obey a requirement; question Q2 records this
  reading). An answer obeying every individually satisfiable requirement is accepted without a
  search; otherwise a search proves no better declaration exists.
- **The solver** (`resolve.decisions.assignment`): pure functions over the model:
  `requirements_obeyed`, `most_obeyable` (a backtracking search, split into independent groups
  of items that share no constraint, with branch and bound on the requirement count), and
  `random_legal(model, rng)` (a randomized search that always returns a legal answer, not
  uniformly). The engine validates with it; `RandomAgent` answers with `random_legal`; the text
  agent and a future AI read the same model. **Risk:** the worst case is exponential in the size
  of a linked group. A benchmark test (30 attackers and 30 blockers with menace and Prized
  Unicorns) must validate in well under a second; M3.5 measures it.
- **Why the space and not `check_answer`:** an agent must be able to find a legal answer from
  the request alone (random agents would otherwise retry blindly into `MAX_ATTEMPTS` against a
  Lure), and a UI must show why a declaration is illegal. Nothing in the model is hidden
  information.

### 3.3 Declaring attackers (507, 508)

- **507.1** has no choice: every multiplayer game uses the attack multiple players option (802).
  The combat state is created as the combat phase begins, with its defending players.
- **508.1**, `DeclareAttackers` (a turn-based action task), for `defaults.attack_declarer` (5):
  1. a `DECLARE_ATTACKERS` decision: a `ChooseAssignment` whose choices are the defending
     players (and, from M4, their planeswalkers and the battles they protect). Two players: one
     target, so the decision is only which creatures attack. 508.1b's announcement is the pick;
  2. 508.1e: banding (M9) would be announced here;
  3. 508.1f: the chosen creatures without vigilance tap (`PermanentsTapped`, not a cost);
  4. 508.1g-j: if any pick is costly, the total cost is locked in and paid with an embedded
     `PayCosts(purpose=ForAttack)` and mana abilities (508.1i). The task takes a checkpoint
     before 508.1f; a cost that can't be paid reverses the declaration (508.1, 733) and asks
     again;
  5. 508.1k: `AttackersDeclared` (each attacker and its target) makes the attackers;
  6. 508.1m: attack triggers trigger (M4 reads the event).
- **508.8:** `defaults.is_skipped` skips the declare blockers and combat damage steps only when
  the combat state says no creature was declared or put onto the battlefield attacking.
- **Seats.** An attacker whose defending player leaves the game stays attacking but deals no
  damage to them (800.4e). A player who leaves removes the creatures they own (800.4a), so they
  leave combat (3.9).

### 3.4 Attack and block legality (101.2) **(design.md §17.2)**

`AttackAction(creature, target)` and `BlockAction(blocker, attacker)` join `allowed`:

- **the rules' permission:** to attack, a creature the active player controls, untapped, not a
  battle, and not `summoning_sick(..., ATTACK)` (508.1a, 302.6, 702.10b), attacking a defending
  player (or their planeswalker or battle); to block, an untapped creature, not a battle,
  controlled by a defending player, blocking a creature attacking that player, their
  planeswalker or battle (509.1a, 802.4a). These are conditions of the permission, not
  prohibitions, so M5's "can block as though untapped" (Masako) adds a permission;
- **prohibitions:** defender (702.3b); "can't attack" and "can't block", with or without
  "unless [condition]" (Topiary Stomper); evasion read from the attacker (flying 702.9b, reach
  702.17b, intimidate 702.13b, fear 702.36b, landwalk 702.14c against the defending player's
  lands, "can't be blocked", "can't be blocked by [filter]" (Juggernaut's Walls), shadow,
  horsemanship and skulk in M9); protection (702.16f, j, k); Champion of Lambholt's power
  comparison; from M5, effects with durations ("can't block this turn").

509.1b: evasion is cumulative because every prohibition applies. Blocks are checked when
declared; later changes don't undo them (509.1b, Champion's ruling).

### 3.5 Declaring blockers (509)

- Each defending player, in APNAP order (802.4), declares in a `DECLARE_BLOCKERS` decision for
  `defaults.block_declarer(state, player)`: items are their untapped creatures that may block
  something; choices the attackers attacking them; maxima `defaults.blocks_allowed` (1, 509.1a;
  Palace Guard: any number). Creatures attacking other players and other players' blockers are
  ignored (802.4b).
- 509.1d-f: block costs as 508.1g-j (`ForBlock`). 509.1g-h: `BlockersDeclared` (each blocker and
  the attackers it blocks, and each attacker blocked or not) makes the blockers and fixes
  blocked status; an attacker stays blocked when its blockers leave (509.1h).
- The event carries what 509.3a-g's triggers need (M4): who blocked whom, and which attackers
  weren't blocked ("attacks and isn't blocked").

### 3.6 Combat damage (510): `DistributeEach` **(design.md §17.8)**

- **Amounts:** each attacker and blocker assigns `defaults.combat_damage_amount` (its power,
  510.1a; Doran and Belligerent Brontodon assign toughness, M5); 0 or less assigns nothing.
- **Who assigns:** `defaults.combat_damage_assigner(state, creature)`: its controller (510.1;
  banding hands it to the other side, 702.22j-k, M9). The active player assigns first, then
  each defending player in APNAP order (510.1, 802.5).
- **Recipients:** unblocked: what it attacks (510.1b), nothing if that's gone or a player who
  left (800.4e); blocked: its blockers (510.1c), nothing if none remain unless it has trample
  (702.19d: all to what it attacks); a blocker: the attackers it blocks (510.1d).
- **The decision:** one `ASSIGN_COMBAT_DAMAGE` per player, covering every creature of theirs
  with more than one possible assignment, in a new space:
  `DistributeEach(parts, constraints)`, one `Distribute` per creature (recipients, total, minimum
  0), answered with one `Amounts` per part. Its constraint `LethalBeforeExcess(part, blockers,
  remaining, deathtouch, excess)` encodes trample (702.19b): damage reaches an excess recipient
  only once each blocker is assigned lethal damage, counting damage already marked and damage
  from the player's other creatures in the same assignment (702.19b); with deathtouch any
  nonzero amount is lethal (702.2c). It is one decision because 702.19b and 510.1e check the
  total assignment, not each creature's. A creature with one possible assignment isn't asked
  (design.md §6, canonical answers), so a creature blocked by one creature without trample, and
  an unblocked one, never are.
- **510.2:** all assigned damage is dealt at once: one `ApplyEvents` of `DamageDealt` events,
  one per source, with `combat=True`. Combat damage from a commander in a Commander game raises
  `NotImplementedRule("903.10a")` until M7 tallies it.
- **510.4, first strike and double strike:** as a combat damage step begins, if an attacker or
  blocker has first strike or double strike, the step deals only their damage, records them
  (`CombatState.first_strike`) and inserts a second combat damage step directly after it into
  `TurnState.segments` (500.9). The second step deals the damage of those that had neither as
  the first began, and of those that have double strike now (702.4b).
- **Deathtouch:** `DamageDealt.apply` sets `GameObject.deathtouch_damage` for a creature dealt
  damage by a source with deathtouch (last known information, 702.2e; any zone, 702.2d);
  704.5h destroys it if its toughness is above 0; each state-based-action check clears the
  flags. Noncombat damage too (Chandra's Ignition).
- **Lifelink:** in the 120.3 table (120.3f, 702.15b): the source's controller (its owner if it
  has none) gains that much life, recorded on the applied event, one gain per source (702.15e).
  Noncombat damage too.

### 3.7 Keywords **(design.md §17.11)**

Ability values in `resolve.cards.keywords` (`evergreen`, and `combat` for the evasion family),
read only through computed characteristics (so M5's granted keywords need no change):
`FirstStrike`, `DoubleStrike`, `Trample`, `Deathtouch`, `Lifelink`, `Flying`, `Reach`,
`Vigilance`, `Menace`, `Defender`, `Haste`, `Intimidate`, `Fear`, `Landwalk(quality)` (a land
type, or 702.14a's type and supertype combinations, as a quality of lands). Shadow, horsemanship,
skulk and banding are M9 (S and X tiers); the design above has their place. Redundant instances
(702.x "multiple instances") are read with `has_keyword`.

Non-keyword combat statics, in `resolve.cards.primitives.combat`: `CantBeBlocked`,
`CantBeBlockedBy(filter)`, `CantAttackOrBlockUnless(condition)`, `CantAttackOrBlockAlone`,
`AtMostCreaturesAttackAndBlock(n)`, `AttacksEachCombat`, `BlocksEachCombat`,
`MustBeBlockedByAll` (Lure's and Prized Unicorn's ability), `BlocksAnyNumber`,
`PowerBlockRestriction` (Champion's), `AttackTax(cost)` (Ghostly Prison), `BlockTax(cost)`.
They are collected from the permanents on the battlefield, as M2.9 collects cost modifiers
(docs/decisions.md 2026-10-27); M5 adds those of resolved effects with durations.

### 3.8 Summoning sickness and haste (302.6, 702.10)

`defaults.can_tap_for_cost(state, obj)` becomes `defaults.summoning_sick(state, obj, purpose)`,
`purpose` being `TAP_ABILITY` ({T} or {Q} in a creature's own ability's cost, 302.6, 702.10c) or
`ATTACK` (302.6, 702.10b). It is true for a creature that hasn't been under its controller's
control continuously since their most recent turn began (M2.4's `entered_turn` and
`last_turn_began`) and lacks haste. It is the one place "as though it had haste" effects plug in
(Thousand-Year Elixir, `TAP_ABILITY` only, M5). Convoke, improvise, crew, saddle and "tap an
untapped creature you control" never call it (docstring; the coverage-map notes are updated).
The M2 tests that named `can_tap_for_cost` are renamed with it, not loosened (question Q5).

### 3.9 Entering attacking, leaving combat, combat timing

- **508.4, `PutOntoBattlefieldAttacking`:** the zone change carries `attacking`: the controller
  chooses which defending player (or planeswalker or battle) it attacks as it enters
  (`CHOOSE_ATTACK_TARGET`, not asked with one choice), unless the effect says. It is attacking
  but never "attacked" (`declared=False`); declaration requirements and restrictions don't apply
  (508.4c); entering during the declare blockers step or later, it is unblocked (508.4d); a
  noncreature, a creature under a non-attacking player's control, or a gone target makes it
  never attacking (506.3a-c, 508.4a-b).
- **506.4, `remove_from_combat(state, obj)`:** the one function, called as a permanent leaves the
  battlefield; control changes (M5), phasing (M9), regeneration (M6) and stopping being a
  creature (M5) call it too. `end_combat(state)` removes everything (511.3); ending the turn or
  the combat phase (724.1d, 724.2d, M9) calls it.
- **506.8, combat timing:** `resolve.rules.combat.combat_point(state)` answers "before or after
  attackers or blockers are declared, in which combat" (506.8a-f, with `TurnState.combats_begun`
  for 506.8d). Tested with a fixture; casting restrictions read it when their first real card
  arrives.

### 3.10 Extra turns, phases and steps (500.7-500.10) **(changes design.md §4)**

- **Phases get a number (question Q1).** `Segment` becomes an Immutable value
  `Segment(phase, step, number)`: segments with the same `number` are one phase of the turn.
  `turn_structure` numbers the normal five; an added phase takes `TurnState.next_phase_number`.
  "After this phase" is found by number, "that combat" (World at War, M4's delayed triggers) can
  name one, and 724.2 can skip the rest of one. The rest of `TurnState.segments` is unchanged.
- **Effects** (`resolve.cards.primitives.effects`):
  - `ExtraTurn(player)` (500.7): pushes onto `GameState.extra_turns`; several players' in APNAP
    order; the most recently created is taken first (M2.1's `next_turn`).
  - `AddPhases(after, phases)` (500.8): inserts after the specified phase (this one, by number);
    several after the same phase, the most recently created first. An added main phase is a
    postcombat main phase (505.1a). "After this main phase" outside a main phase adds nothing
    (Relentless Assault's ruling).
  - `AddStep(step, after_or_before)` (500.9) and `AddStepAfterPhase(step, after)` (500.10:
    creates that step's phase with only that step).
  - "You get" on another player's turn adds nothing (500.10a).
  - `UntapAll(filter)` with the filter "attacked this turn" (508.6), from
    `resolve.rules.history.attacked_this_turn`, the first query of M4's log-derived history
    (question Q6): the creatures still on the battlefield as the same objects that were declared
    as attackers this turn.
- Each combat phase has its own combat state, so 508.8 is decided per combat, and
  "attacks each combat if able" applies in each (508.1d's last sentence).

### 3.11 Views, agents and tools

- `PlayerView.combat`; the text renderer shows attackers, targets, blockers and blocked status;
  `TextAgent` answers `ChooseAssignment` (per creature, its picks) and `DistributeEach`.
- `RandomAgent`: `ChooseAssignment` by `random_legal`; `DistributeEach` by a random division per
  part, redrawn on a broken `LethalBeforeExcess` (at most `MAX_ATTEMPTS`; the canonical
  "lethal first, rest anywhere" draw guarantees termination).
- The auto-payer (`resolve.agents.autopay`) pays attack and block costs from
  `PlayerView.payments` (`ForAttack`, `ForBlock`; 106.6 restrictions don't allow such spending
  unless they say so).
- `tests/combat_support.py`: `declare_attackers`, `declare_blockers`, `assign_damage`, like
  `casting_support.py`.

## 11. Questions (approved as proposed, 2026-10-30)

- **Q1 (design.md §4 change): numbered segments.** `TurnState.segments` holds
  `Segment(phase, step, number)` instead of `(phase, step)` tuples, so "after this phase",
  "that combat" and 724.2 can name one phase among several of the same kind. Alternative
  rejected: phase-boundary markers in the list (every reader must skip them).
- **Q2 (design): declarations as compiled constraint models** in `ChooseAssignment` (3.2), with
  validation, requirement maximization and a legal-answer search in the space itself, shared
  with agents; `DistributeEach` as a new space for damage assignment (3.6). Also the reading of
  508.1d recorded in decisions: the maximum is taken over declarations that pay no cost, and a
  declaration that pays costs must obey at least that many requirements.
- **Q3 (cards):** the 14 keyword example cards, the 7 combat-rules cards, Time Walk and
  Relentless Assault, and Zetalpa (section 7), with Nacatl Savage excluded from the fuzz.
- **Q4 (torture tests):** M3 implements the Lure row's rules with Prized Unicorn and the
  Basilisk Collar row's with a fixture; the rows stay open until Lure and the Collar themselves
  join the tests in M5.
- **Q5 (rename):** `can_tap_for_cost` → `summoning_sick(state, obj, purpose)`, one rule-default
  function for both halves of 302.6.
- **Q6 (look-back):** "attacked this turn" read from the event log as the first query of M4's
  history (design.md §17.9), rather than a per-turn record like `lands_played`.
- **Q7 (protection's damage):** keep 702.16e a gap until M6, with fuzz exclusions; the
  alternative is pulling protection's prevention into M3, ahead of the prevention stage it
  belongs to.
- **Q8 (lifelink):** lifelink's life gain recorded inside `DamageDealt` until M6 needs results as
  separate events (120.4c), as M2 did for 120.3a's life loss.
